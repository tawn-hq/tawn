> Antler, Techstars, EF, local funds, etc.), plus a general guide to what these
> applications ask for and how to answer well.

## How to use this doc

1. Grab the right-length blurb from §1 and paste it where the form asks
   "describe your company."
2. Use §2 to answer the standard questions almost every application asks.
3. Run the §3 checklist before you hit submit — it's what separates a strong
   application from a vague one.
4. §4 is the honest-numbers worksheet you must fill in; never leave traction
   blank — write what's true, even if early.

## 1. Reusable blurbs (copy-paste)

### One-liner (≤ 15 words)
**Tawn is one private, local-first memory that all your AI tools share — across your
work, money, research, and studies.**

### Twitter/elevator (≤ 50 words)
**Every AI tool forgets you and works in a silo. Tawn is a local-first "digital
twin": one private context core that your existing agents (Claude, Gemini, Cursor,
and more) all read from and write back to — unifying your work, wealth, research,
and academic life, and keeping itself up to date so it never feeds them stale
context.**

### Standard paragraph (≈ 100 words)
**AI assistants fail at memory in three ways: they don't know who you are, they
don't know what you know, and they don't know what they just did — and your life is
split across four unconnected tools for work, money, research, and study. Tawn is a
local-first personal "digital twin": one private memory core that every AI agent
you already use shares through an open standard. It links your domains with an
entity graph (so your PhD proposal can draw on your shipped work and papers at
once), maintains its own knowledge so it never goes stale, and keeps your data on
your machine. Open-source core, hosted and team versions to come.**

### Long form (≈ 250 words)
**The problem.** Knowledge workers now use several AI tools every day, and each one
is amnesiac and siloed. They don't remember your context, your knowledge, or what
they (or another agent) just did. Worse, your life spans four disconnected
domains — work, wealth, research, academic — so no tool can reason across them, and
the few "second brains" that exist quietly go stale and start misleading you.

**The product.** Tawn is a local-first personal digital twin: a single private
memory core that every AI agent you already use reads from and writes back to,
through the emerging `AGENTS.md` + MCP standards. It unifies your domains with a
shared entity graph, auto-compiles your notes into a cross-referenced knowledge
base that gets sharper with every source, and federates memory from your tools and
cloud AI apps into one brain — then projects that brain back out so every agent
behaves consistently. It's private by default (data stays on your machine, hybrid
local/frontier model routing) and safe by design (read-only core, explicit
permission grants, human approval on anything that acts).

**Why it wins.** Prior art exists only in pieces; nobody ships the *multi-domain*
twin with a shared graph and self-maintaining knowledge. That integration is the
moat, compounded by an open-source community and an integration marketplace.

**Where it's going.** Open-source single-user core first, then hosted/multi-user
and team versions, plus a marketplace of domain and action integrations. «Add your
traction + team line here.»**

## 2. Standard application answers (pre-filled)

**What does your company do?**
See §1. Lead with the 50-word version.

**What's the problem?**
Three universal AI memory failures (contextual, knowledge, operational) plus
four-domain fragmentation plus stale "second brains." Everyone using ≥2 AI tools
feels this daily.

**What's your solution / why is it better?**
One shared, self-maintaining, agent-agnostic memory core with a cross-domain entity
graph — not another chatbot or a single-domain note app. It makes the tools people
*already use* smarter instead of replacing them.

**Why now?** (timing is what accelerators score hardest)
- Local models crossed a real capability threshold in early 2026 (Qwen3-Coder,
  Llama 4 Scout, DeepSeek V3.2) — private, on-device AI is finally good enough.
- `AGENTS.md` became the shared cross-agent instruction standard; MCP became the
  tool standard — so one core can drive every agent.
- Agent proliferation made memory fragmentation a daily, acute pain.

**Who's the customer?**
Initial: multi-domain technical knowledge workers (engineers, researchers,
founders) who already live across several AI tools and value privacy. Expansion:
researchers, operators, and eventually teams.

**Market.**
Sits across personal-AI / second-brain / AI-memory-infrastructure. «Add a TAM/SAM
estimate and 2–3 comparable market sizes.» Wedge: privacy-conscious power users;
expand to teams and hosted.

**Business model.**
Open-source self-hostable core (free, drives adoption + trust). Revenue: hosted
cloud version (for non-Linux + multi-user/teams), premium integrations, and a
marketplace of domain/action integrations (take rate). «State pricing hypothesis.»

**Go-to-market.**
Developer-led, open-source distribution; ship in public; integrate with the agent
ecosystems users already use; community-built integrations widen the moat.

**Competition / moat.**
Pieces exist (Khoj, Goose, Vellum/OpenClaw, Mem, Rewind/Limitless, Personal.ai) but
each is single-domain or single-tool. Moat = the multi-domain shared memory + entity
graph + agent-agnostic federation + self-maintaining knowledge layer, compounded by
open-source community and an integration marketplace, and defended by local-first
privacy.

«How much, for how long of runway, and the 3 things it buys (e.g. hires, hosted

**Vision (3–5 years).**
The default private memory layer for personal AI — every person owns one twin their

Maintenance/staleness of the knowledge layer (mitigated by the self-compiling wiki)

## 3. Pre-submit checklist

- [ ] One crisp sentence anyone non-technical understands.
- [ ] "Why now" tied to ≥2 concrete 2026 shifts (you have them above).
- [ ] Real traction stated (users, stars, design partners, demos, waitlist) — §4.
- [ ] A **2–3 min demo video** + a link to try it (or the repo). This matters more
      than the deck.
- [ ] Team slide: why *you* specifically (you already build the RAG stacks Tawn
      reuses — say that).
- [ ] Clear, specific ask and use of funds.
- [ ] Market framed with a defensible wedge, not "everyone."
- [ ] Distinctive insight stated plainly (multi-domain shared graph nobody ships).

## 5. Tips that win these applications

- **Show, don't tell.** A working demo and a public build beat adjectives.
- **One sharp insight.** Yours: *everyone patches one memory failure; the win is one
  shared brain across all your domains and all your agents.* Repeat it.
- **Concrete "why now."** Timing is the most-scored, least-faked answer.
- **Founder-market fit.** You're building the tool you need and already have the RAG
  components — make that explicit.
- **Be specific about the ask.** "Raising «$X» for «N» months to do «3 things»."
- **Keep it honest.** Strong programs reward clarity and traction signal over hype.
